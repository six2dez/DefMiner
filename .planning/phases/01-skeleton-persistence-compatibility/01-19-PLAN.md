<objective>
Close the last three CORE-11 blindnesses — WR-23, WR-26 and IN-20 — and mark the requirement complete
against an executed gate rather than against an intention.

Purpose. Plan 01-18 closed the receiver key. Three shapes remain where the gate's stated reach is wider
than its executed reach, and all three are the phase's signature defect at smaller scale:

```
eval(s)                                       ["outbound-dynamic-code"]
const e = eval; e(s)                          []        <- WR-23
const { eval: ev } = globalThis as any; ev(s) []        <- WR-23
new Function("a", s)                          ["outbound-dynamic-code"]
const F = Function; new F("a", s)             []        <- WR-23
const W = WebSocket; new W(url)               []        <- WR-23, constructor half
sdk[o.length].send(req)                       []        <- WR-26, `length` assumed numeric by NAME
const g = globalThis; g.fetch(u)              []        <- IN-20
const g = globalThis; g["fet"+"ch"](u)        []        <- IN-20
```

**WR-23 is the sharpest of the three and its docblock is why.** The dynamic-code rule's own comment
tells the reader it reaches as far as the receiver rules beside it — "including a bare member REFERENCE
with no call, the same way the receiver rules already catch `const s = sdk.requests.send`". It reaches
one spelling less far than any of them. `fetchAliases` is seeded with the global and grown from three
spellings; `navigatorAliases` mirrors it exactly; the dynamic-code rule was written between them and
given neither. And this is the surface that reaches every other surface in CORE-11's enumeration,
through a string no AST gate can read into.

**WR-26 is a claim about a claim.** `isProvablyNumeric`'s docblock states the discipline in as many
words — it "PROVES rather than assumes and it fails SAFE" — and its property-access branch declares any
expression ending in `.length`, `.size`, `.min`, `.max` numeric regardless of what it is a member OF.
`{ max: "requests" }.max` is a string. A numeric key short-circuits `isAssembledKey` before any other
test runs, so this branch decides what the receiver rules never get to see. The exposure today is nil —
neither scanned root indexes an outbound receiver by a member-named key — which is why it is a warning
and not part of CR-08. The exposure the day somebody relies on the exemption is silent, and the docblock
is what they will rely on.

**IN-20 is two lines and it is the same asymmetry one level out.** `navigatorAliases` and `fetchAliases`
both resolve one hop; the receiver they sit on does not.

**SEVERITY, STATED HONESTLY.** All three are prospective. Nothing leaks today, the gate runs green over
the real tree, and `check:bundle` reports one specifier. These are gates that would stay green on call
sites written later. No sentence written by this plan may imply a live leak.

**And then the ledger.** CORE-11 was reverted from `[x]` to `[ ]` at commit `e7cc4b6` because CR-08
contradicted its Complete status. This plan owns marking it complete again — but only against an
executed gate, and only once its text and the gate agree on one bound. That order is the whole point: a
checkbox flipped before the last enumerated shape has a fixture that has been observed failing is the
arithmetic that revert undid.

Output: one alias mechanism reused three times, a docblock that is true of its function, a numeric
exemption that establishes what it claims, and a requirement marked complete against evidence.

**Why wave 19, and why serialised.** It shares `outbound-prohibition.spec.ts` with plan 01-18 one wave
back, and it must not flip CORE-11's checkbox until 01-18's receiver-key fix has landed and its
disclosures agree. Beyond the file overlap, the reason every gap-closure plan in this phase since 01-09
has been serialised applies unchanged: this plan mutates the shared working tree to prove its gates can
fail while asserting the whole suite green, and a concurrent plan's red is indistinguishable from its
own.

**What this plan deliberately does NOT do.** It does not revisit the receiver key — plan 01-18 owns
CR-08 and this plan asserts its fixtures still pass rather than re-deriving them. It does not touch
`packages/backend/src/store/*` (plans 01-20 and 01-21 own WR-22 and WR-24), `tests/pins.spec.ts` or
`scripts/phase1/tracer-e2e.sh` (plan 01-22 owns WR-25 and IN-21). It does not touch
`scripts/ci/check-bundle-imports.mjs`, which answers "measured loadable" and not "permitted" (decision
P9-D2). It does not run a live Caido. It modifies no plan 01-01 through 01-18, and no
`01-VERIFICATION.md`, `01-REVIEW.md` or `01-UAT.md`.
