<objective>
Make the tracer's one-page walk into a safe, sustained, observable backfill: the watermark that keeps
live browsing winning, the bounded skip-done read that stops the scan reloading work it already did,
the descending multi-page loop with its yield and its re-entrancy flag, and the separate retro
counters that keep OBS-01's live numbers meaning what they say.

Purpose: an unthrottled producer provably discards live traffic — `BoundedQueue.offer()` drops
OLDEST at cap, which is correct for live traffic and exactly wrong under a backfill. And a 40,000-request
backfill that folds its rejections into the live counters makes the shipped drop count and reject
reasons stop describing live proxying.

Output: a producer that can run for hours without costing the operator a single live entry, and
counters that say which caller they are serving.
</objective>

**The watermark gate.** Import `SCAN_BACKPRESSURE_WATERMARK` and `SCAN_PAGE_SIZE` from
`@defminer/engine/thresholds` — never a local literal, the rule `consumer.ts` already follows for
`RETENTION_SWEEP_EVERY_N`. Before issuing a query, read the queue's current depth: offer only while
depth is STRICTLY below the watermark. At or above it, do not query, set the producer's
`heldAtWatermark` flag true, yield, and re-check. Record why the comparison is strict and why the

queue drops OLDEST at cap, so an unthrottled backfill would provably discard the live entries the hook
just admitted.

watermark and a scan whose QuickJS thread is blocked both show counters that stop advancing, and
without the explicit signal the readout's `Waiting for the analysis queue` state can never render and
every legitimate hold falls through to the stall marker.
