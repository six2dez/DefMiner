<objective>
Discharge D-06: prove, over a captured fixture corpus, that the HTTPQL clause pushed down to
`sdk.requests.query()` is a SUPERSET of `admit()`'s kind axis — and prove it with a suite that can
fail.

Purpose: the push-down is an optimisation and `admit()` is the gate, so an over-matching clause costs
bandwidth and an under-matching clause costs artifacts the operator will never learn were missed.
D-06 is the decision that a disagreement must be a red test rather than a silent hole, and research
found a concrete one already: there is no response content-type field in HTTPQL at all, and the
extension operator is documented case sensitive while the shipped classifier lowercases before its
suffix test — so an uppercase script path is admitted by DefMiner and missed by the obvious clause.
That is not a hypothetical; it is the reason this plan exists.

Output: a generated fixture corpus, a disposable in-plugin probe that records Caido's own verdict per
fixture, a live harness that captures the corpus once, a schema-validated result artifact, and a
vitest gate that joins the recorded verdicts to the shipped classifier and asserts the implication —
with the non-vacuity negative `06-VALIDATION.md` requires.

**Why this cannot be one in-process test.** `sdk.requests.matches()` is a plugin-runtime method: it
does not exist in node and cannot be faked without inventing an HTTPQL engine, which would make the
suite a test of the fake. `isScriptish` is ordinary TypeScript and is only meaningful if it is the
shipped module, which the plugin bundle does not re-export. So the proof is split at the runtime
boundary — Caido's evaluator records its verdict per fixture into an artifact, and the gate joins that
artifact to the shipped classifier by fixture id. Neither half is a re-implementation of the other.
</objective>
