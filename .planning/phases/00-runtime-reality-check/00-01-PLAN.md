  <name>Task 2: SPIKE-02 — does setTimeout(fn,0) actually yield, and does external RSS sampling attribute correctly</name>
  <files>probe/tier0-core/backend/script.js, scripts/spike/rss-sampler.sh, tests/spike-results.spec.ts, .planning/phases/00-runtime-reality-check/results/SPIKE-02.json</files>

SPIKE-02 asks in words: *does `setTimeout(fn, 0)` actually yield the QuickJS event loop?* If it does not, budget-and-background does not work and the entire ingestion design in CORE-06 and CORE-07 must change. This spike runs before every other measurement in the phase because its answer shapes the measurement loop of everything that follows.

Write `scripts/spike/rss-sampler.sh` from the research: poll `ps -o rss=,%cpu=` for a PID at a configurable interval defaulting to 50 ms, emit `unix_ms,rss_kb,cpu_pct` with millisecond Unix timestamps, and stop when the process is gone. The Unix-ms timestamp is the correlation key against the host log's microsecond-resolution lines and against the probe's own markers; measured agreement in the research was 0.26 ms.

Extend `probe/tier0-core/backend/script.js` with three registered functions.

First, `yieldExperiment` in the corrected fixed-duration form from the research. The methodology matters more than the code here: a fixed **iteration count** produced a false negative during the research because a 50-iteration run completed in 0.355 ms, shorter than a single 4 ms timer period. So the loop runs for a fixed wall-clock window, counts a `setInterval` at 4 ms as the service-opportunity signal, and returns both the observed tick count and the count theoretically possible, plus a `valid` flag that is false when too few periods elapsed to observe anything. Run it across four candidates — `setTimeout(r, 0)`, `setImmediate(r)`, `Promise.resolve()`, and a fully blocking baseline with no yield — over a 300 ms window with a 1 ms synchronous slice, and report the service ratio for each. Then measure the per-yield cost of the winning primitive over at least 40 samples and report the median and p95. Note that `setTimeout` clamps to at least 4 ms in this runtime, so `setTimeout(fn,0)` and `setTimeout(fn,1)` are indistinguishable; record the clamp observation rather than assuming it.

Second, `yieldAtGeometry`: repeat the winning primitive's measurement at the real CORE-06 geometry — 64 KB chunks with 4 KB overlap over a multi-megabyte string, with representative synchronous work in each slice rather than a spin loop — and report the total yield overhead for an 8 MB input under two policies: yield per chunk, and yield when elapsed synchronous time approaches a 25 ms slice budget. The research projects roughly 730 ms of pure overhead for the per-chunk policy versus about 19% for the temporal policy; confirm or correct those numbers at the real geometry. This is what sets MAX_SYNC_SLICE_MS, and it is why CORE-06 says the yield trigger is temporal while the chunk geometry stays for matching-window reasons.

Third, `rssAttribution`: emit a start marker, allocate and retain roughly 32 MB of JS strings, emit a marker, retain another 64 MB, emit a marker, then drop every reference and emit a final marker. Every marker carries `Date.now()` and `performance.now()`. This exists to validate the only memory-measurement technique available in this runtime before SPIKE-06 depends on it — there is no in-runtime introspection at all, `llrt:qjs`, `perf_hooks` and `process` all fail to load, `performance` carries only `now` and `timeOrigin`, and there is no `gc()`.

Run all three against a fresh instance on port 8999 with the RSS sampler attached to the Caido PID for the whole run. Correlate the sampler CSV to the markers by timestamp and compute, for the attribution run, the ratio of observed RSS delta to bytes allocated at each step, plus the RSS delta after references were dropped. Record explicitly that RSS did not fall — it is a high-water mark, the allocator does not return pages, and any later assertion of the form "memory after is at most memory before" is invalid in this runtime. Carry that correction into the result's `if_wrong` and `notes`, because QUAL-06 in Phase 11 depends on it.

Write `results/SPIKE-02.json` with a `measurements` array carrying one `timer_service_ratio` entry per primitive, the `yield_cost` median and p95 with its sample count, the two geometry policy overheads, and the attribution ratios.
